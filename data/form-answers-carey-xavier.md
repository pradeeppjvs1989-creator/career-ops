### "Your strengths"

> I make abstract science visible, and then I get students' hands on it. My lessons are built around
> custom animations and visual sequencing so the theory lands quickly, which buys back class time for
> practical work. That is the whole point of the visual layer for me. It is not decoration, it is how
> I protect lab time.
>
> I also work from evidence rather than instinct. My most recent cycle of inquiry with a Year 7 class
> set success criteria before the unit started and measured against three separate instruments, not
> one test. The class median moved from a 51% baseline to 70%. It was not uniform, and the student who
> went backwards taught me more than the ones who improved.
>
> The third strength is that I share. The dissection resources, the Standard and PLP versions of
> assessments, the rocketry unit. Colleagues use them. I would rather lift a department than have a
> good classroom on my own.

> Medium term, curriculum leadership. I have already written and submitted two full elective proposals

## PART D — The question that catches you out

Independent schools almost always ask this one, and it is your genuine gap. Have this ready:

### "What co-curricular contribution would you make?"

> I will be honest here, because you will find it out anyway. I have not coached a team or run a club.
> My contribution at my current school has been the unofficial kind: I ran a voluntary lunchtime
> science session, with the Head of Education's approval, for students who were not even in my classes
> and who came looking for help. I gave them my own notes and my own time.
>
> What I would bring is a science club or a competition team, and I would build it properly rather
> than babysit it. Science Talent Search, a robotics or rocketry group, a Biology olympiad squad. I
> designed a rocketry unit this year that a colleague adopted, so I know I can make that kind of thing
> run.
>
> I am also willing to take on sport or a Saturday commitment and learn it. I would need someone to
> teach me, and I would rather say that than turn up claiming experience I do not have.

**Why this works:** it converts a gap into a specific offer plus a real proof point. Do not soften it
into "I am enthusiastic about co-curricular involvement". That reads as nothing.
